Weather Fronts

Three states, one wind, and a million tiny splashes. The weather machine eases the world between calm, breezy and storm — greying the sky, breaking the crests into foam, and drumming rain onto the disturbance bus. Force a storm and watch everything answer at once.

Scroll down to read ↓

In Fish Hawk, weather is the difficulty dial that never says so. A calm morning is a tutorial: glass water, fish glints everywhere, easy lift. A breezy afternoon makes the ridge lift generous but hides the fish under chop. A storm grounds sensible birds entirely — a missed dive costs daylight, and there is less of it. The machinery behind that dial is today's build, and it is deliberately small: a state machine with three states and a rule that nothing ever snaps. Fronts arrive. The sky from part one, the clouds from part three, the lake from The Mirror — none of them will learn a single new trick today. They will just be told different numbers, slowly.

Step 1 · The machineTargets, not values

Each state is nothing but a table row of targets: 

calm    wind  1.2 m/s   gust 0.30   overcast 0.06   rain   0/s
breezy  wind  5.5 m/s   gust 0.55   overcast 0.34   rain   0/s
storm   wind 11.0 m/s   gust 0.85   overcast 0.94   rain 260/s

The machine's live values chase those targets with per-quantity time constants — wind shifts in about six seconds, the sky greys over nine, rain arrives in three because a squall line really is a wall — using the standard exponential ease v += (target − v)·(1 − e^(−dt/τ)). Then the two write-backs that make it the world's weather rather than a demo's: the eased wind speed and gustiness are written into the spine's wind — the one field every system samples — and the overcast value lands in the atmosphere's uniform. In auto mode the machine walks calm → breezy → storm → breezy → calm with randomized dwells. Watch the bars chase the orange target ticks:

Step 2 · WhitecapsThe lake reports the wind

Sailors read wind off the water, and the Beaufort scale is the codification: scattered whitecaps near 4 m/s (force 3), frequent by 7, streaks of blown foam past 11. Our crests should break on the same schedule. The wave table from Wind over Water already scales amplitude with wind speed; whitecaps are a fragment-stage question asked of the same waves: is this point on a crest, and is the wind past breaking? Height normalized by the wind-scaled amplitude says "crest"; the slope magnitude says "steep" — a breaking wave is both — and a smoothstep gate on wind speed, opening at 3.5 m/s and saturating near 10, says whether crests break at all. Aside · Why foam is white

Lake water absorbs red and transmits blue-green — that was the depths post. Foam is the same water shattered into bubbles: thousands of air–water interfaces scattering all wavelengths equally before absorption gets a word in. White is what scattering looks like when it wins. (The same argument made the clouds white last post — foam is a cloud you can land on.)

Step 3 · RainA million tiny splashes, one channel

Rain is where the disturbance bus earns its keep. Every drop that reaches the lake is one honest event: disturb({kind: "rain", x, z, energy: ~0.001, radius: 0.05}). The machine carries a fractional accumulator — 260 drops a second at 60 fps is about 4.3 per frame, and the remainder rolls over so the long-run rate is exact. Nothing about the emitter knows what listens. Today the listener is The Mirror's ring pool, turning each event into a spreading capillary ring; next season the fluid sim will inject real momentum and the audio series will synthesize patter from the very same events, emitter untouched. Step 4 · Storm lightThe world flattens before it darkens

What does an overcast value of 0.94 do? Three things, all downstream of one idea: the cloud deck swallows the direct sun and re-emits its energy as diffuse skylight. The sky color collapses toward a luminance-keyed grey (keyed, not constant, so storm noon stays brighter than storm dusk). The sun's disc and glow fade with (1 − overcast)³. And the direct-light color every glint and slope reads is starved down to a tenth, while ambient holds — which is why an overcast world looks flat before it looks dark: the ratio of key to fill is the first casualty. Aside · The storm that grounds the bird

Real ospreys sit out heavy weather — wet feathers fly badly, and rain ruins the view of the fish through a churned surface. In the game, storm targets feed straight into the flight model's wind term and the spotting system's noise floor, so "too rough to hunt" emerges from the numbers instead of a scripted lockout. The dial never says difficulty; it says weather.

OnwardWhat this unlocks

The hero up top is the whole post running at once: the machine cycling, clouds thickening on cue, crests breaking, rain ringing the bus, lightning resetting the exposure of the world for a heartbeat. One machine, five readers, zero special cases. What the weather has not touched yet is the far side of the clock — the hours after the front clears, when the sky goes truly dark and a different light source takes the stage.
